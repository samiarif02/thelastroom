**Duolingo onboarding (2024–2026): findings**

- **Screen order.** Welcome (Duo waves) → pick a language → "How did you hear about us?" → "How much [language] do you know?" → "Why are you learning?" → daily goal → notification opt-in → first lesson or placement test → streak start → set a streak goal. This is the order a search-result summary gave. I couldn't confirm "how did you hear" in the teardowns I opened, so treat its position as unverified. Source: https://userguiding.com/blog/duolingo-onboarding-ux (it also lists 7 signup steps, with the account step last and name/age asked afterwards).
- **Motivation and level questions.** One question per screen. Motivation tiles are things like Travel, Career, Brain training. Level is "Learning for the first time" vs "I already know some". Source: https://junoschool.pages.dev/article/duolingo-onboarding-experience/
- **Daily goal.** Four tiers: Casual 5 min, Regular 10, Serious 15, Intense 20 (about 10 to 50 XP). Source: https://duolingoguides.com/how-to-change-daily-goals-on-duolingo/
- **Value before signup.** Users personalise and play the core lesson first; signup comes "at a later stage… even after having played with the core app". Source: https://theappfuel-migrated.pages.dev/casestudies/three-learnings-from-duolingos-onboarding. A quick translation exercise runs "before asking users to commit… with a signup". Source: https://goodux.appcues.com/blog/duolingo-user-onboarding
- **Placement test.** Only shown if the user says they aren't new. It is adaptive: it "starts off easy and gets harder according to performance". Source: goodux link above.
- **Length.** About 4 minutes to finish onboarding in 2024 (7 in 2022), and "no more than three to four minutes" to the first lesson. A recent gallery shows a 24-screen flow, and one analysis argues a ~60-screen flow wins because every screen is a small step forward. Sources: the junoschool link above and https://screensdesign.com/articles/duolingo-onboarding-design/
- **Tone.** Duo speaks in speech bubbles, a progress bar sits at the top, and there are cute micro-animations. Rows of correct answers trigger combo popups; lesson ends say "Niiice!". Sources: https://screensdesign.com/articles/duolingo-onboarding-design/ and https://growth.design/case-studies/duolingo-user-retention
- **Home path.** A winding path of circles, one per lesson, grouped into small named units ("get directions"). Lessons unlock in order. Completed lessons are gold and can be replayed. Locked ones are grey; tapping one says what unlocks it. A floating arrow jumps back to your current spot. Practice nodes sit inside the path. Source: https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design. The bouncing "START" bubble on the current lesson is something I know from the app; no source I fetched confirms it.
- **Reward after a lesson.** A "Lesson complete!" screen with a big Duo illustration and confetti, stat tiles for total XP, accuracy and speed, and a Claim XP button, followed by the streak flame. Streak milestones turn Duo into "Fire Duo", treated like a game power-up, with a share card. Sources: https://screensdesign.com/apps/duolingo-language-lessons/ and https://blog.duolingo.com/streak-milestone-design-animation

**What to borrow for interview-practice onboarding**

- **Structure.** One question per screen, big tappable tiles, a thin progress bar at the top, and a host character's speech bubble asking each question. The host reacts after each answer, e.g. "Senior PM? Good. They'll push harder."
