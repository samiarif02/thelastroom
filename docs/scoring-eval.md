# How well does the AI recruiter score? (evaluation)

Generated by `supabase/eval/score/run.mjs` on 2026-11-02 against the live `score`
function with the owner's Gemini key (brief 36). Re-run it after any change to the prompt, the
models or the content; publish the result as it comes, good or bad.

## The method

- **21 answers**: for each of the 7 Chapter 1 missions, a *strong* answer (the
  strongest line of every beat, the principle that really explains it, the best reply), a *weak* one
  (the weakest lines, a tempting wrong principle, the worst reply) and a *guessed* one (strong
  lines picked faster than anyone could read them, no reasons). Built from the content by the
  app's own request builder, so the AI sees exactly what a player's answer sends.
- **The expected band** for each kind is the content author's intent: strong 70–100, weak and
  guessed 0–50. **The engine's score** for the same play is the offline reference.
- Each answer is scored **3 times** (temperature 0) to measure consistency.

## What we changed because of it (2026-11-02)

- **Every verdict timed out on the first live run.** `gemini-3.8-flash` answered 503 "high demand"
  or took 30 s+ and allows no minimal thinking. The function now tries a chain of faster models
  with minimal thinking, and the app swaps a late verdict in for the offline score (ADR 0006).
- **Guessed answers scored too high** (60 for an answer whose three lines were all guessed, against
  a band of 0–50). The prompt now caps an all-guessed answer at 40 overall and 20 specificity;
  re-scored: 25–40.
- **A weak line with a good-sounding reason scored too high** (65 for Mission 6's blaming answer).
  The prompt now says a good reason never rescues a weak line; re-scored: 45–55.
- **The key is on Gemini's free tier**: per-model rate limits (429) cause most of the missing
  verdicts here. The app falls back to the offline score when that happens; production needs
  billing on the key.

## Honest limits

- The bands are our own intent, not an outside benchmark: this shows the AI agrees with the
  rubric we wrote, not that the rubric matches every recruiter.
- 21 answers is small; it catches a broken prompt or a model swap, not subtle bias.
- Temperature 0 narrows the spread but doesn't remove it; progress over time (the baseline, the
  report) stays on the engine for that reason (ADR 0006).
